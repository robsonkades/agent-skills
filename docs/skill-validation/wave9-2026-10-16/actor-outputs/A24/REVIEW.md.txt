# Decision: reject the proposal as written

The supplied packet is an authored fixture. It supports rejecting the proposed accounting and recovery conclusions; it does not establish production behavior. No policy or implementation was changed.

**Configured limits.** All attempt limits include the initial attempt. Nested retry layers multiply: current maximum `2 outer × 3 SDK × 1 gateway = 6` wire attempts per logical call; proposed maximum `2 × 3 × 3 = 18`, an increase of 12, not two. These are nominal configured ceilings, not observed counts or latency guarantees. The packet supplies no numerical combined deadline, phase bounds or backoffs, so reachability and elapsed-time bounds cannot be calculated. A shared shrinking deadline would have to clip every retry layer; cancellation alone does not supply that bound.

**Observed counters.** In the same completed cohort, outer amplification is `1060 / 1000 = 1.06`; wire amplification is `1600 / 1000 = 1.6`. Each outer attempt invokes one SDK operation. Consequently, `1600 - 1060 = 540` additional wire sends are SDK retries, as the packet explicitly attributes them. There are 60 outer retries. The 600 sends beyond one per logical call split into those 60 outer retries and 540 SDK retries. SDK sends average approximately 1.5094 per SDK operation.

**Token accounting.** The bucket admits outer retries, not individual wire sends. Four processes start with `4 × 10 = 40` tokens; 900 qualifying successes contribute at most `900 × 0.1 = 90`. With no restarts or other refills, the aggregate grant upper bound is **130**. The observed 60 grants are consistent with that necessary aggregate constraint. Local saturation, event ordering and uneven traffic can reduce usable credits; aggregate counters cannot establish that every process implemented its bucket correctly. The `4 × 20 = 80` maximum balance is instantaneous capacity, not a lifetime grant cap. Even the outer-attempt bound for this cohort is at most `(1000 + 130) / 1000 = 1.13`, not an unconditional 1.1. A 0.1 refill ratio therefore neither caps fleet wire amplification at 1.1 nor makes the observed 1.6 evidence of a broken bucket.

**Effects and cancellation.** At 50 ms the timeout/cancel signal leaves operation-41's effect unknown. At 52 ms, a current-state negative lookup cannot rule out later application. The fixture then explicitly records the original write committing at 80 ms. Thus the final supplied evidence establishes that original effect, although its response delivery and resource-release time are unspecified. There is no acknowledgement or release timestamp proving cancellation stopped the work. Retain the original identity, reconcile the commit, and do not declare definitive failure or issue a replacement intent. No replay guarantee is supplied; merely reusing a key would not authorize automatic redispatch either.

**One next verification.** After correcting the arithmetic and withdrawing automatic redispatch, run one isolated deterministic replay against the revised recovery path: one intent, virtual time 0–100 ms, a 20-second process cap, and exactly the packet's timeout/negative-lookup/late-commit sequence. Require UNKNOWN at 50 and 52 ms, zero replacement or repeat dispatches, and reconciliation of the original intent to COMPLETED when the 80 ms commit evidence arrives. Passing would change this rejection to acceptance of that revised, bounded local recovery behavior and corrected accounting; it would not authorize production guarantees. The unchanged proposal cannot pass by observation because its arithmetic is false. This check is proposed, not executed.
