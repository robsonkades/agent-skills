# Proposal review

**Decision: revise; do not ship this proposal.** The supplied authored incident packet contradicts its attempt arithmetic, budget interpretation and effect-safety claims.

| Quantity | Value | Meaning |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Configured maximum before | 6 | 2 outer × 3 SDK × 1 gateway attempts |
| Configured maximum after | 18 | 2 outer × 3 SDK × 3 gateway attempts |
| Observed outer attempts per logical call | 1.06 | 1,060 / 1,000 |
| Observed wire attempts per logical call | 1.60 | 1,600 / 1,000 |
| Aggregate outer-retry grant upper bound | 130 | 4 × 10 initial tokens + 900 × 0.1 refill |

These maxima include initial attempts and describe nested configured limits, not measured sends or attainable latency. Adding a gateway layer multiplies the existing maximum; it does not add two sends. A deadline or retry admission may prevent reaching those limits. No numerical combined deadline or phase/backoff durations are supplied, so an end-to-end latency bound cannot be calculated.

The bucket admits **outer retries**, not SDK sends. Its 0.1 refill ratio therefore establishes no fleet-wide 1.1 wire-amplification cap. Initial tokens also invalidate that finite-cohort cap even at the outer layer. The four local buckets start with 40 tokens and can receive at most 90 more in this cohort. Per-process capacity 20 may discard refill tokens, and distribution/timing can reduce usable grants. Aggregate instantaneous capacity 80 is not the cumulative grant limit. The observed 60 grants equal 1,060 − 1,000 and fall below 130; this is consistent with the supplied budget, although aggregate counters cannot prove every local bucket correct.

Each outer attempt invokes one SDK operation. The aligned cohort therefore contains 1,600 − 1,060 = **540 additional SDK wire attempts**, beyond the 60 additional outer attempts. This explains why the 1.60 wire ratio does not diagnose a broken outer bucket. The proposed gateway configuration has no observed cohort here.

At 50 ms, timeout and a cancellation signal leave operation-41's effect **unknown**. The 52 ms lookup observes only current absence; the packet expressly supplies no guarantee against later application or safe replay. At 80 ms the original write commits, resolving this fixture's effect as committed, 30 ms after caller timeout. There is no cancellation acknowledgement or resource-release timestamp. Neither a fresh intent nor an automatic same-key replay is justified. Preserve operation-41's identity and reconcile its eventual result; timeout is not terminal business failure, and commit time is not a resource-release measurement.
