## Accepted SLO and evidence

The population is the fixed cohort of **all 10,000 eligible logical operations started**, with no exclusions. An operation is good only if the caller receives the required successful result within **100 ms**. At least **99%**, or **9,900 operations**, must be good. Retry attempts are not additional logical operations. The stated demand is offered load; these artifacts do not independently supply admitted or attempted rates, so no conversion to successful operations/s is claimed.

The start-side census is complete and the outcome counts cover that same cohort. All selected instances served `fixture-v1` with the tested resource settings. The defect concerns missing outcome evidence, not a mixed-configuration population.

| Cohort category | Operations | Interpretation |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Known good | 9,800 | Successful caller outcome within 100 ms |
| Known bad | 0 | No known failures or deadline misses in the supplied counts |
| Missing terminal records | 200 | Timely outcomes cannot be reconstructed after collector loss |
| Eligible population | 10,000 | Complete SLO denominator; no operations excluded |

The dashboard computes `9800 / 9800 = 100%` over recorded terminal events. That is a rate conditional on having a terminal record, not the accepted all-starts SLO. Removing the 200 unobserved outcomes from the denominator does not demonstrate compliance. There is no evidence that missingness is random or that the missing outcomes resemble the observed ones.

Let `x` be the number of genuinely good operations among the 200 unknown outcomes. Then:

```text
0 <= x <= 200
actual good fraction = (9800 + x) / 10000
lower bound = 9800 / 10000 = 98%
upper bound = (9800 + 200) / 10000 = 100%
required good count = ceil(0.99 * 10000) = 9900
SLO holds if and only if x >= 100
```

The bounds straddle 99%. At least 100 of the 200 unknown operations must actually have been good for this cohort to pass; 100 good and 100 bad among them would exactly meet the SLO. Fewer than 100 good among them would fail. Neither outcome is established. These are deterministic missing-data bounds, not statistical confidence intervals. In particular, **98% is a lower bound, not an observed failure rate**; collector loss does not make the 200 operations known timeouts or known deadline misses.

This point provides neither an established passing lower bound at 1000 operations/s nor a failing upper bound on service capacity. Do not infer a capacity interval, add replicas, or declare a performance regression from it.
