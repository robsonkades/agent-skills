## Accounting bound and consequence

Let n = 10,000 eligible operations, g = 9,800 known good, u = 200 unresolved,
and q = 0.99. The counts reconcile: 9,800 + 0 + 200 = 10,000.

`g / n <= realized good fraction <= (g + u) / n`

Thus the realized good fraction is bounded by **[98%, 100%]**. The 99% threshold
lies inside this interval: the lower bound does not establish PASS, and the
upper bound does not establish FAIL. Treating 98% as the observed actual rate
would incorrectly classify every lost record as bad. Treating the dashboard's
100% as the cohort rate would incorrectly discard unresolved eligible work.

Passing requires at least 9,900 good operations: at least 100 of the 200
unresolved operations must actually have been good. Up to 100 total bad
operations are compatible with the SLO; 101 would make this cohort fail. The
supplied records cannot determine which occurred. Resolving at least 100 of the
missing outcomes as good would establish the count criterion even if the rest
remained unknown; establishing at least 101 as bad would establish failure.

These are deterministic accounting bounds for this cohort, not a confidence
interval, a reconstructed p99, or proof of future capacity. The observation does
not establish a passing or failing load point, a capacity bracket, or a safe
alternative replica count. Preserve the original records and the historical
dashboard result as evidence, but exclude this point from the accepted feasible
envelope until its classification is supported. No numerical forecast was
supplied to revise; any inference that relies on this point being feasible must
remain provisional.
