Call OrderService and MetricsService through their configured Spring beans. Inspect the corresponding datasource after the call completes.

OrderService.place explicitly selects ordersTx. Its order insert, stock reservation and
synchronous local OrderNotifier validation complete in one orders-database transaction.
A stock or notifier rejection rolls back both writes and propagates the original failure.
Success returns after transaction completion. The notifier remains a local participant;
there is no asynchronous follow-up or recovery queue.

MetricsService intentionally retains unqualified @Transactional and the primary metricsTx
manager. A failed sample rolls back its metrics-store write independently of orders.
Do not switch the primary manager to repair an orders failure.

The transaction-active diagnostic alone cannot identify which datasource is enlisted.
Previously the orders operation opened a metrics transaction while its orders JDBC writes
autocommitted. Both rejection exceptions were already unchecked; rollbackFor and a new
proxy/self-invocation workaround were unnecessary.

Run `node check.mjs` for the incident reproduction (expected: active=true, committed orders=0).
Run `node check.mjs work.Regression` for five assertions covering stock rejection, notifier
rejection after both writes with cause preservation, successful reservation, database
insertion failure and unchanged metrics commit/rollback. Tests call actual Spring beans
without enclosing test transactions and inspect persisted state after completion.
